Technician job card

Product design · Android app · 2 days

An Android app so technicians stop writing down repairs and parts by hand. Technicians see their next job, managers see which jobs are stuck, and riders know when to pick up their scooter.

Context

Blive rents electric scooters to delivery riders. Every hour in the workshop is an hour the rider can't earn.

Repairs are tracked on paper and WhatsApp photos.

Three problems I picked

  • Waiting for parts is invisible, so it looks like slow work.
  • Costs are pieced together later, so nobody trusts the invoice.
  • Logging is extra work on a shared phone with greasy hands, so it gets skipped.

Research

I did desk research on three user personas: the technician, the rider and the service manager.

The cost is real: riders lose about ₹1,000 a day off the road, and 48% can't afford a day off a week.

What each persona needs

  • Rider → time. Riders get a pickup date on WhatsApp. If a part runs late, they get a new date and the reason.
  • Manager → exceptions. Managers see which jobs are stuck and why, with photos and parts behind every cost. They get alerts for delays and cancellations.
  • Technician → what's next. Technicians see their next job, what the rider said, and which jobs are ready to start repair.

The core job: know what's next, log it without typing, and flag when you're waiting on a part.

Then I mapped the flow, from the job being created to the scooter being ready for pickup.

My first wireframes had the right steps but vague words. "TBD: 2 days" could mean the part, the fix or the scooter, so I changed it to a date.

Assumptions and decisions

  • Parts database. Every EV part is listed with its cost and how long it takes to reach the garage. The app shows which parts are already in the garage and which are a couple of days away.
  • Pickup date = part ETA + 1 day to fix. I'm assuming a technician needs about a day to finish a job once the part arrives. If a job needs several parts, the last one to arrive sets the date. If it needs no parts, the scooter is ready the same day.
  • The manager creates every job. This comes from the brief.
  • Quality check on every job. Services get one too.

Design decisions

  • Damage photos come with part requests. I made parts requestable once a technician adds photos of the damage, so the manager can see why each part is needed.
  • Dash scan. I chose to read the odometer and battery level from a photo of the scooter's dash, so nothing has to be typed.
  • Every Next saves to the phone. I made every step save on the phone, so if the signal drops, nothing has to be filled in twice.

Left out, and why

  • Inventory, tools and screws. These are an inventory problem, not a job card one, so they're out of scope. The app only tracks the EV parts a job needs.
  • Timers and micromanagement. I left these out to keep accountability without surveillance, so technicians don't have to log breaks. Every tap is time-stamped and every job has photos and parts, so the app doesn't need to watch anyone.
  • Guided photo angles. Technicians already know what to shoot, and a strict checklist would turn the check into a chore.

System

  • I tried a dark Cred-style look and an Airbnb-style one, then went plain and light for a bright workshop.
  • The type is Be Vietnam Pro at −3% letter spacing on a 4px scale, with 52px buttons for greasy thumbs.
  • Colour only means status: red is urgent, blue is waiting and green is ready.
  • Buttons name the real next step, like "Mark as repaired" instead of "Done". Nothing is done until it passes the quality check.

I tested the flow against three jobs: an urgent brake failure, a job whose parts just arrived, and a routine maintenance check with no damage or parts required. I also added a path for closing a job that can't be finished.

Screens

Technicians share one phone, so they pick their name (recent ones first) and enter a PIN. The Today screen has two tabs: Jobs, with urgent jobs pinned on top, and Repaired, which shows each quality check result.

The check has three steps: photos, a dash scan, then damage. With no signal, photos save on the phone and send later. An optional voice note explains the damage and helps the manager approve parts.

Parts are grouped by area of the scooter. Ordering a part updates the rider's pickup date. When the part arrives, the job skips the check and opens straight on repair.

If the quality check fails, the reason comes back as a voice note with photos. The technician can fix it again, order more parts, or close the job with a reason. The manager decides what happens next.

Stuck? The Help button on every job screen calls the manager.

Test plan

Five technicians use the prototype on real jobs. I'd track three things:

  • Fewest updates. Count how many times the technician has to update the app per job. Fewer is better.
  • No WhatsApp about parts. Each WhatsApp message about parts is information the app failed to carry.
  • Riders feel it's on time. Track how many scooters are ready by the pickup date, and ask riders one question at pickup: was it ready when we said?
